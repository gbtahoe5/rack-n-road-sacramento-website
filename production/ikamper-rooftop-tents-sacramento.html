Vehicle & System Foundation

What sits under the tent matters as much as the tent itself.

A premium rooftop tent is only as good as the foundation underneath it. Most of the things that decide whether the system feels right at year three are decided before the tent ever goes on the vehicle.

Roof load — static is the easy number, dynamic is the real one

Your roof can hold the tent at rest. The question is what it carries on the freeway with crosswinds, and what it supports with two adults and gear up top at camp.

Crossbar compatibility — bars, towers, and feet

Not every crossbar is rated for a hardshell. Spacing, bar shape, and tower mounting all change the answer. Wrong bars under a great tent is a slow, expensive problem.

Hatch clearance and ladder angle

Six inches forward, your tailgate opens. Six inches back, it doesn't. The same decision dictates whether your ladder lands on level ground at most campsites, or on a slope every time.

Daily-driver reality

An iKamper adds about a foot to your roofline. Drive-thrus, parking decks, and whether the tent fits in your own garage at home — those are install decisions, not tent decisions.

Long-term usability — living with the system

Installation isn't only about today. It's about how the tent feels to load, deploy, drive with, and use repeatedly two and three years from now.

Common Mistakes

The decisions people regret six months in.

None of these are catastrophic on their own. But they each take a little off the ownership experience — and the tent costs too much for that.

Mistake 01

Buying the tent before thinking through the rack system

The tent shows up. The bars don't fit it. Now you're either driving without a tent or paying twice for a foundation you should have planned in week one.

Mistake 02

Underestimating total weight

Tent plus mattress plus bedding plus people. The tent's spec weight is one number; the system you actually deploy at camp is another.

Mistake 03

Assuming factory bars are enough

Some are. Many aren't. Almost no one makes this judgment well from a manufacturer's website alone.

Mistake 04

Ignoring garage clearance

An iKamper roughly adds a foot to your roof. Measure your garage door before you commit, not after.

Mistake 05

Forgetting daily vehicle height

Drive-thrus, parking structures, the auto-height-clearance bars at the office. The tent lives on your vehicle every day, not just on trips.

Mistake 06

Choosing for appearance instead of long-term usability

The look matters — but only the people who don't own one yet weight it that heavily. Owners weigh how it feels to live with.

Mistake 07

Not planning for accessories

Awning, annex, lights, storage. Most people add at least two within a year. The system should be ready for that on day one.

Mistake 08

Not asking how it'll feel two years in

What's it like to load? Deploy? Park with? Use repeatedly? The answer shouldn't be "we'll figure it out."

Vehicle & Fit8 questions

Will an iKamper work on my vehicle?
Most pickups, mid-to-large SUVs, vans, and overland-oriented platforms work well. Compact cars and some crossover bar setups don't. Send us your year and trim or bring it in — we'll give you a real answer in five minutes.
Are my factory bars enough?
Sometimes, sometimes not. We check the bar type, the manufacturer's static and dynamic load ratings, and the spacing across your roof. We'll tell you honestly whether yours are up to it.
Do I need new bars or a platform?
Often the bars; sometimes a platform; occasionally neither. A platform makes accessory mounting and weight distribution noticeably easier — but it isn't always required.
What's static vs. dynamic load?
Static is what your roof can hold parked (tent, gear, people inside). Dynamic is what it carries while driving — much lower, usually 150–200 lbs. Both have to work for the system you want.
What about a panoramic or sliding sunroof?
Tents go on bars, not glass — so a sunroof is mostly fine. We check that the bars clear the roof opening and that the tent's weight won't change how the sunroof tracks behave over time.
Can it go on a pickup with a bed rack?
Excellent setup. Bed racks distribute load over the truck's frame, lift the tent above the cab, and give you a deck to work from. One of our most-recommended foundations.
Will it work on a Sprinter or Transit van?
Ideal — vans and platforms were made for each other. Long roof, flat surface, plenty of capacity. Usually we're matching to an aftermarket platform you already have or planning one in.
What about lifted vehicles or oversized tires?
Workable, but the math changes. A lift adds height before the tent does. We'll measure your garage, your typical parking, and the climb to the ladder before recommending anything.

Sizing & Clearance6 questions

Will it still fit in my garage?
An iKamper adds roughly 12 inches to your roof. Measure your garage door clearance plus a small buffer for sag and snow load. Under 7 feet, we'll talk through options before you commit.
How much height does it add?
About 12 inches at the highest point of the closed shell, slightly more with bars and feet. We'll give you the exact number for your specific tent and bar combination.
What about parking decks and drive-thrus?
Most parking decks are 6'8" — too low for a vehicle with a rooftop tent. Drive-thrus vary. Some customers plan their daily routes; others don't drive theirs daily. Worth thinking about up front.
Can I take it through an automatic car wash?
Avoid touchless ones with overhead beams, and definitely no brush washes. Hand wash is the safe move. We can recommend wash spots in Sacramento that work.
Will it whistle or hum on the freeway?
Usually no — iKamper's hardshell is quieter than most soft tents. The right bar and tower system makes the difference. Wrong bars or wrong placement is what creates whistle.
How much does it affect fuel economy?
Typically 1–3 mpg on a mid-size SUV at highway speed. Hardshells like iKamper hurt mpg less than soft-shell or hybrid designs because they're aerodynamic when closed.

Living With It7 questions

How fast is setup at camp?
Hardshell deploys in under a minute solo, faster with a partner. Take-down is comparable. Fastest setup in the category — which is why owners actually use it.
How does it handle wind and rain?
Well, when set up correctly. Hardshell construction handles wind better than canvas. Rain depends on the rainfly being properly stretched and the tent being level. We walk through both at handover.
Is it warm enough in cold weather?
With proper bedding, yes — down to about freezing. Below that you want an insulator pad, a 3-season-plus bag, and a propane heater rated for ventilated spaces if you're cold-sensitive.
Can I leave bedding inside when closed?
Yes — one of the best parts of a hardshell. Bedding stays put when the tent folds. Setup at camp is unzip and climb up.
What about condensation?
Some condensation is normal in any tent. iKamper's BreathWeave fabric handles it well. The bigger lever is ventilation — keep windows or vents cracked, even when it's cold.
Will it leak in heavy rain?
A properly set-up iKamper handles serious weather. Most "leaks" we see are setup issues — rainfly not pulled taut, a window left ajar, the tent set up at an angle that pools water. We cover this at handover.
How do I clean it?
Brush off debris, rinse with mild soap and water, let it dry fully before closing. Avoid harsh detergents and pressure washers. We can recommend a specific cleaner that works.

Accessories & Add-ons4 questions

What accessories should I plan for now?
First-year adds: awning, annex, lighting, storage (fuel, traction boards, recovery gear). You don't have to buy them all at once, but the install should anticipate them — otherwise you'll be redoing brackets later.
Can I add an awning or annex later?
Yes. The awning system mounts to the side of the shell. The annex zips to the tent's footprint. Both are easy to add after the fact if the bars and platform were planned for it.
Can I add solar or LED lighting?
Lighting is straightforward. Solar takes a bit more planning. A platform makes both easier. We'll spec wiring, switches, and battery if you want a complete system.
Can I mount bikes or kayaks alongside?
Often yes, depending on the foundation. Bed racks and platforms create deck space for additional accessories. We've run a tent, kayak, and bikes on the same Tacoma — it requires planning, but it's possible.
