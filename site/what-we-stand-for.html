Honest advice

Your age matters. We say it because no one else will.

This is one of the most useful conversations we have with customers, and it almost never happens online. The rack that makes sense at 35 may not make sense at 60. Lifting a 40-pound bike over your head onto a roof rack is a different ask at different points in your life — and we'd rather have an honest conversation about it now than watch you stop riding because the rack got hard.

Hitch-mounted platform racks have transformed the experience for an enormous number of our customers. Lower lift, easier load, less strain. There is no shame in choosing the rack that keeps you outdoors longer. That's the entire point.

Weight, leverage & torque

The physics matter more than the marketing.

A rack doesn't just carry weight — it multiplies it. The farther your gear sits behind the hitch, the harder it levers your vehicle. Estimate your real system load:

At this load you are almost certainly looking at a 2″ receiver and a heavy-duty platform rack. Let us confirm your vehicle's rating.

*Leverage estimate, not a safety rating. The straight-down weight does not change — but a load carried farther back levers the hitch harder: roughly double the distance, double the strain. Hitches are rated with the load close to the receiver, and reaching well past that point can cut usable capacity by up to about half. Bumps and braking spike it higher still. We verify your vehicle's and hitch's actual ratings in person.

Total weight is the first question, not the last.

Leverage works like a leverA loaded rack sits well behind the receiver, so its weight acts on a lever arm. Double how far it reaches back and you roughly double the bending strain on the hitch — which is why two racks of the same weight can behave very differently.
The lowest-rated component winsBike, rack, hitch and vehicle each carry a rating. The smallest one controls the system. A 100-lb rack on a 1.25″ Class 2 hitch is still limited by the hitch.
EVs, hybrids & batteriesBattery placement changes what your platform can handle, and some racks only hit their rating with the battery removed. Where it lives also changes where a clamp can safely grip.

Practical, not promotional

Cargo boxes: should you buy or rent?

This is one of the few times we'll talk a customer out of a purchase. The right answer depends on how often you'll really use it — not how often you imagine you might.

Rent if…

You take one or two long road trips a year, you have nowhere to store a 7-foot box, or you're not yet sure if your travel pattern is going to stick. Our rental program exists for exactly this customer.

Buy if…

You ski every weekend, you travel with kids and gear three or more times a year, or you've already rented twice and know the box pays for itself. At that point, a quality box is a 10-15 year investment, not a seasonal expense.

The most misunderstood category

Bike racks aren't a product. They're a system.

More customers regret a bike rack purchase than any other rack we sell. Almost always, it's because the decision was reduced to "two bikes or four" — when the real variables are bike weight, frame geometry, lift height, and how the system fits the life of the person using it.

The rack you'll actually use is the one that's easy to use.

A rack that takes ten minutes to load is a rack that gets left in the garage. We've watched it happen for 35 years. Ease of loading isn't a luxury feature — it's the single biggest predictor of whether you'll still be riding in three years.

E-bikes change the math

A 55-pound e-bike isn't the same problem as a 22-pound road bike. Frame, hitch class, and tongue weight all need to be re-evaluated.

Frame style matters more than you think

Step-through, full-suspension, carbon — each one rules certain racks in or out. A hanging-style rack on the wrong frame is a scratched paint job waiting to happen.

Lift height is a silent dealbreaker

If you can't comfortably lift the bike onto the rack, you'll stop using it. Platform-style racks at the right height change everything.

FAQ

Common questions, answered honestly.

How do I know if I need a hitch-mounted rack or a roof rack?
It comes down to lift height, vehicle, and what you're carrying. Heavier bikes, e-bikes, and frequent loading almost always favor a hitch platform. Roof racks remain unmatched for paddleboards, kayaks, and roof boxes. We'll walk you through it on your specific vehicle.
Can I move my rack to a new car?
Often, yes — especially the platform itself and the accessories. Crossbars and feet may need a new fit kit. Hitches are vehicle-specific. We'll tell you exactly what carries over before you buy.
Do you install racks I bought somewhere else?
In most cases, yes. We install hitches, racks, and accessories regardless of where they were purchased, and we'll let you know up-front if there's a fit or compatibility concern.
How long does an install take?
A bare rack swap can take 30 minutes. A hitch and wiring install on a modern SUV typically runs 1–3 hours. We'll give you a precise estimate when you book.
Should I rent a cargo box first?
If you're not certain how often you'll use one, yes. Renting once or twice answers the question better than any spec sheet. If you start renting more than twice a year, buying usually pays off quickly.
Are e-bikes safe to carry on most racks?
Only on racks specifically rated for them, mounted to a hitch class that can handle the tongue weight. This is one of the most common mistakes we correct in the shop. Talk to us first.
